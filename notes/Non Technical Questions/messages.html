Non Technical Questions
## 🔹 1. Basic Introduction Questions

These are *almost guaranteed*:

* Tell me about yourself
* Walk me through your resume
* Tell me about your background

👉 Tip: Keep it structured → Present → Past → Future

---

## 🔹 2. Project & Experience Questions

Very important for you as a developer:

* Explain your project
* What was your role in the project?
* What challenges did you face?
* How did you solve a difficult problem?
* What would you improve in your project?
* Which project are you most proud of?

👉 Tip: Use STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result)

---

## 🔹 3. Strengths & Weaknesses

Classic HR filter:

* What are your strengths?
* What is your biggest weakness?
* How are you improving your weakness?

👉 Tip:

* Strength → real + backed with example
* Weakness → safe + improving

---

## 🔹 4. Behavioral Questions

They test how you think and act:

* Tell me about a time you faced a challenge
* Describe a failure and what you learned
* Tell me about a conflict in your team
* How do you handle pressure?
* Have you ever made a mistake?

👉 Tip: Always show learning + growth

---

## 🔹 5. Team & Work Style

Very common in product companies:

* Do you prefer working alone or in a team?
* How do you handle disagreements?
* What kind of work environment do you prefer?
* How do you prioritize tasks?

---

## 🔹 6. Career & Goals

They check your seriousness:

* Why do you want this job?
* Why should we hire you?
* Where do you see yourself in 3–5 years?
* What are your career goals?

👉 Tip: Align your goals with the company

---

## 🔹 7. Company-Focused Questions

Check if you researched:

* What do you know about our company?
* Why do you want to join us?
* Why are you leaving your current job?

---

## 🔹 8. Salary & Availability

Usually at the end:

* What are your salary expectations?
* When can you join?
* Are you open to relocation?

---

## 🔹 9. Situational Questions

Hypothetical scenarios:

* What will you do if your deadline is near but task isn’t complete?
* How would you handle a difficult teammate?
* What if you don’t know how to solve a problem?

---

## 🔹 10. Reverse Question (Very Important)

They almost always ask:

* Do you have any questions for us?

👉 Always ask something like:

* “What does success look like in this role?”
* “What are the biggest challenges in this team?”

---

## 🔥 Pro Tip (Important for You)

Since you're preparing for product companies:

Focus heavily on:

* Tell me about yourself
* Project explanation (deep clarity)
* Challenges + problem-solving
* Why this company

🔹 II. Project & Experience Questions

1. Explain your project

In my previous role, I worked on a shipping domain-based ERP application for a logistics company called Global Feeder Shipping. The application is used to manage vessel operations, port activities, and various cost calculations involved in shipping.

The main goal of the system is to streamline operations and accurately calculate different types of port-related charges that ship vendors need to handle.

From a technical perspective, the application is built using Java and Spring Boot for the backend, Angular for the frontend, and PostgreSQL as the database.

My role as a Java Full Stack Developer involved working on both backend and frontend. On the backend, I developed REST APIs, implemented business logic for cost calculations, and handled database interactions. On the frontend, I worked on integrating APIs and building UI components for displaying data.

One of the key features I worked on was the cost calculation module, where multiple types of charges were calculated based on different parameters. Initially, the logic was repetitive and hardcoded, so I refactored it into a scalable and configurable solution by moving configurations to the database and creating a reusable service. This made the system easier to maintain and extend.

I was also involved in debugging issues, handling edge cases in API responses, and optimizing certain queries for better performance.

Overall, I worked on building and improving scalable, maintainable features and ensuring smooth integration between backend and frontend components.

4. How did you solve a difficult problem?

One difficult problem I worked on was related to performance, where some modules had delayed API responses, causing slow UI loading.

I started by analyzing the flow and identified that multiple API calls were being executed sequentially, and some database queries were not optimized.

To solve this, I optimized the backend queries to reduce unnecessary data fetching, and on the frontend, I used parallel API handling using forkJoin so that multiple calls could be executed simultaneously.

After implementing these changes, the response time improved significantly, and the UI became much more responsive.

This experience helped me understand how to identify performance bottlenecks and optimize both frontend and backend effectively.

🔹 IV. Behavioral Questions

Answers :
=========

1. Tell me about a time you faced a challenge ?

In one of my projects, which was a shipping application, we had multiple types of costs that needed to be calculated for ship vendors. The existing implementation had repetitive logic for each cost type, with values hardcoded in the code, which made it difficult to maintain and extend.

My responsibility was to improve the maintainability and scalability of this module.

To solve this, I refactored the code by creating a common service to handle cost calculations. Instead of hardcoding values, I made the cost configurations dynamic by storing them in the database. The calculation logic was written once and reused by passing different parameters for each cost type.

As a result, the code became more modular and scalable, and adding a new cost type no longer required code duplication—only configuration changes were needed.


~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~


2. Describe a failure and what you learned ?

One situation I consider as a learning experience was when I spent extra time trying to design a solution to be highly scalable, even though the requirement was relatively simple.

Because of this, the implementation took longer than expected, and it slightly impacted the delivery timeline.

From this, I realized that while writing scalable code is important, it’s equally important to balance it with the current requirements and deadlines.

Since then, I’ve improved by first focusing on delivering a simple and working solution, and then enhancing it if needed, based on future requirements.


~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~


3. Tell me about a conflict in your team

In one of my projects, we had a situation where we needed to implement a complex calculation that involved multiple operations and data fetching.

I initially preferred implementing it in Java, as I felt it would be easier to maintain and understand, given the number of steps involved. However, my teammates suggested using a single optimized database query for better performance.

At first, I wasn’t fully convinced, but instead of rejecting the idea, I evaluated both approaches by comparing their performance and maintainability. After testing, I found that the database query approach was significantly more efficient.

Based on that, I agreed with the team’s approach, and we proceeded with the optimized solution.

This experience helped me understand the importance of being open to different perspectives and making decisions based on data rather than assumptions.


~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~


4. How do you handle pressure?

When I work under pressure, I focus on staying structured and prioritizing tasks effectively. I usually break down complex tasks into smaller parts and start with the most critical or manageable ones.

If I get stuck, I don’t spend too much time on a single issue—I discuss it with my teammates or seniors to get clarity and move forward efficiently.

I also take short breaks when needed to stay focused and maintain productivity.

This approach helps me manage pressure effectively while ensuring that I meet deadlines without compromising code quality.

🔹 IX. Situational Questions

Answers :
=========

1. What will you do if your deadline is near but task isn’t complete?

If a deadline is approaching and the task isn’t fully complete, I first analyze what is pending and prioritize the most critical parts that are required for delivery.

I make sure to communicate the status clearly with my team or manager, so there are no surprises. If needed, I discuss possible options like adjusting scope or getting support to complete the task on time.

At the same time, I focus on delivering a stable and working solution rather than rushing everything, ensuring that quality is not compromised.

This approach helps in managing deadlines effectively while maintaining reliability.


~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~


2. How would you handle a difficult teammate?

If I have to work with a difficult teammate, I first try to understand their perspective and the reason behind the difficulty, whether it’s related to communication, workload, or approach.

I focus on maintaining clear and professional communication, and I try to discuss any issues calmly to find a common ground.

If there are differences in approach, I prefer to align based on project requirements and the best possible solution rather than personal opinions.

If the issue still continues and affects the work, I would involve the team lead to ensure smooth progress.

My focus is always on maintaining a positive working environment and delivering the task effectively.


~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~


3. What if you don’t know how to solve a problem?

If I come across a problem I don’t know how to solve, I first try to understand it clearly by breaking it down into smaller parts.

Then I explore possible solutions by referring to documentation, previous code, or other reliable resources, and try to solve it on my own.

If I’m still stuck after a reasonable amount of time, I discuss it with my teammates or seniors to get guidance and move forward efficiently.

Once the issue is resolved, I make sure I understand the solution properly so I can handle similar problems independently in the future.
